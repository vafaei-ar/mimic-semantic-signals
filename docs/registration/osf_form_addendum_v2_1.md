## Study Information

### Research questions

Primary RQ1. Among adult MetaVision ICU stays in MIMIC-III present at a 12-hour ICU landmark, do semantic scores from the latest pre-landmark note improve discrimination of invasive ventilation in the next 12 hours when added to a comparator of structured physiology, treatment and support state, and documentation behavior? RQ2. The same question for renal-replacement therapy in the next 12 hours. RQ3. The same question for ICU death in the next 12 hours. Secondary RQ4. Is any semantic increment retained when only the six state-dominant constructs are used, excluding poor treatment response and escalation considered? RQ5. How does the increment from the eight semantic scores compare with that from a high-dimensional lexical representation (TF-IDF) of the same stripped note, and do the semantic scores add anything once TF-IDF is included? RQ6. Are the primary findings similar when the unstripped note, alternative semantic instruments (Laya, DiffusionGemma), the broad respiratory-support ventilation endpoint, stricter timing rules, and CareVue ICU-death stays are used? RQ7. Does the incremental value disappear when each patient's semantic scores are replaced by those of another patient with similar comparator-predicted risk? Validation RQ8. Do the eight semantic scores agree with blinded clinician ratings of the same constructs in the same notes? RQ9. Does a MIMIC-derived semantic model retain discrimination in an external Chinese ICU cohort (Zigong), using locally translated notes and, separately, native-Chinese notes?

### Hypotheses

All hypotheses are evaluated by estimation, not significance testing. For each primary outcome we will report the difference in AUROC (rich comparator plus Open-Jev minus rich comparator), a 95% patient-cluster refit-bootstrap interval, and the estimates from four further cross-validation partitions. No p-values are calculated, and no outcome is classified as a success or failure according to whether its interval excludes zero. Precision planning showed that the full-cohort analyses can reliably detect increments of roughly 0.011 to 0.024 AUROC, so smaller estimates will be interpreted as imprecise rather than as evidence of no effect. H1 to H3 (RQ1 to RQ3, directional). Adding the eight stripped-note Open-Jev scores to the rich comparator increases AUROC (delta AUROC > 0) for each outcome. Rationale: clinician documentation can record concern and trajectory before they are fully expressed in charted physiology. Earlier exploratory results suggested the increment is outcome-dependent, largest for ICU death and close to zero for renal-replacement therapy, where structured renal data are highly informative. We therefore expect heterogeneity across outcomes and do not expect a positive increment for every outcome. H4 (RQ4, directional). The state-dominant six-construct subset retains a positive but smaller increment than the full eight-score set. A substantially smaller increment would indicate that much of the signal reflects documented management intent rather than patient state. H5 (RQ5, directional). TF-IDF adds at least as much discrimination as the eight semantic scores, and the semantic scores add little once TF-IDF is included. This hypothesis concerns the trade-off between compact interpretable scores and high-dimensional lexical text; it is not a claim that the semantic scores are superior. H6 (RQ6, non-directional). The primary estimates are not materially changed by the listed sensitivity analyses. Differences will be reported descriptively, including any disagreement between MetaVision and CareVue ICU-death results. H7 (RQ7, directional). The patient-shuffled negative control produces a delta AUROC close to zero and smaller than the primary estimate. H8 (RQ8, directional). Model scores are positively associated with clinician ratings of the same construct, and more strongly than with ratings of other constructs. H9 (RQ9, directional). External discrimination in Zigong is above chance but lower than internal discrimination.

## Data Description

### Datasets used

MIMIC-III Clinical Database (primary dataset). MIMIC-III contains deidentified, longitudinal electronic health record data for patients admitted to critical care units at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston, between 2001 and 2012, including 53,423 adult hospital admissions and 38,597 distinct adult patients. It includes charted vital signs, laboratory results, fluid outputs, medication and procedure records, demographics, in-hospital mortality, and free-text clinical notes (NOTEEVENTS). ICU data were recorded in two clinical information systems, CareVue (2001 to 2008) and MetaVision (2008 to 2012). We use adult ICU stays (age 18 or older, neonatal ICU excluded) still in the ICU 12 hours after admission. The unit of analysis is the ICU stay at this 12-hour landmark. Predictors are taken from the 12 hours before the landmark: structured physiology, laboratory values, urine output, respiratory and vasoactive support, sedation, code status, note-documentation metadata, and the latest eligible bedside note (nursing, physician, respiratory, general, or consult). Outcomes are invasive ventilation, renal-replacement therapy, and ICU death in the following 12 hours. The confirmatory analyses use MetaVision stays (11,116 to 19,811 rows depending on outcome). CareVue stays are used only for a separate ICU-death replication (25,632 rows). Critical care database comprising patients with infection at Zigong Fourth People's Hospital, v1.1 (external validation). This is a deidentified database of 2,790 ICU admissions with infection at Zigong Fourth People's Hospital, Sichuan, China, between January 2019 and December 2020. It contains baseline characteristics, laboratory results, medications, diagnosis codes, nursing charts with free-text Chinese nursing descriptions, and follow-up outcomes. We use the nursing narratives and an invasive-ventilation endpoint for external validation of the semantic measurements. The Zigong cohort and endpoint will be rebuilt under the corrected leakage rules described in the technical appendix before outcome labels are joined.

Both datasets consist of routinely collected clinical data recorded during care, not data collected for research. MIMIC-III collection and deidentification are documented in Johnson et al. (2016) and at https://mimic.mit.edu/docs/iii/. Zigong collection is documented in Xu et al. (2022) and on the PhysioNet project page. Note availability, note type, and charting frequency reflect clinical workflow and differ systematically between the CareVue and MetaVision systems, which is why the confirmatory analyses are restricted to MetaVision. Outcome ascertainment depends on charted procedures and recorded death times. The Zigong data come from one Chinese hospital, include only patients with infection, and use Chinese nursing narratives. Both datasets exclude patients without ICU admission.

### Measured variables

Outcomes (binary; 1 = event in the 12 hours after the landmark, 0 = none): Invasive ventilation (primary): first MetaVision intubation procedure (PROCEDUREEVENTS_MV item 224385) after the landmark. Sensitivity endpoints: explicit intubation evidence, and broad respiratory-support evidence. Renal-replacement therapy: first MetaVision RRT procedure (items 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809, 225955) or active RRT charting (items 226499, 227290). ICU death: recorded death time within the ICU stay. Structured physiology comparator (34 features, 12 hours before the landmark unless stated): age (capped at 90), sex; latest value and 6-hour change for heart rate, systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure, respiratory rate, SpO2 and temperature; latest GCS eye, verbal and motor components (airway-coded verbal responses set to missing); latest 24-hour lactate, creatinine, BUN, WBC, hemoglobin, platelets, sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, chloride, glucose, total bilirubin, INR and pH; 6-hour net urine output. Treatment and support context (6 hours before the landmark): latest FiO2 (normalized to percent), latest oxygen flow, high-flow oxygen indicator, non-invasive ventilation indicator, vasoactive infusion active at the landmark and number of active agents (norepinephrine, epinephrine, phenylephrine, dopamine, vasopressin), sedative or analgesic infusion active at the landmark and number of agents (propofol, midazolam, dexmedetomidine, fentanyl). ICU-death comparator only: last code status at or before the landmark, coded as four binary indicators (available, DNR/DNI limitation, comfort or no-CPR, other), with full code as reference. Invasive ventilator settings and airway evidence are not used as predictors. Documentation behavior and note context (note metadata only, prior 12 hours): note count, total and latest note length in characters, number of note-category groups, and counts of nursing, physician, respiratory and other notes; has_note; age of the selected note at the landmark; collapsed category of the selected note. Semantic predictors (primary exposure): eight scores, each a probability between 0 and 1, produced by Open-Jev from the latest eligible bedside note with prespecified direct treatment and endpoint language removed: overall clinician concern, worsening trajectory, respiratory concern, hemodynamic concern, poor treatment response, escalation considered, diagnostic uncertainty, reassuring stability. Notes are split into chunks. Each score is the maximum across chunks, except reassuring stability, which uses the minimum. Scores are used individually, not summed into an index. The six-construct state-dominant subset omits poor treatment response and escalation considered. No factor analysis is used. Lexical reference: TF-IDF of the same stripped note (unigrams and bigrams, at most 10,000 features, fitted within training folds only). Categorical coding: binary variables are coded 0/1. The collapsed note category is one-hot encoded with an explicit no-note level. Code status uses the four indicators above. No source-system indicator is used. Instrument specification. Open-Jev: model com-kotobalabs/open-jev-deberta-v3-large, Hugging Face revision 19bf9a64815add579fbf6c907bef584d9277a8e4, run locally with the typed-decisions library at commit 10d7834d3b99041f890db4615fb38ef95ced50cc; notes are split into 220-token chunks with 40-token overlap, at most 8 chunks. Laya: package version 0.3.4, 600-token chunks with 100-token overlap, at most 8 chunks. DiffusionGemma: google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it, Hugging Face revision f7f5b7f5fa82ffc52addd066915886d497f5517b, run locally through Hugging Face Transformers with chunk size derived from the model context limit, at most 8 chunks. Construct questions and response criteria are those in src/semantic_schema.py (SHA-256 72763082c314a4542817ccfcd42d2b10d9446fc8e84c3391a2140790b2483623). Chunk scores are aggregated as the maximum across chunks, or the minimum for reassuring stability. These settings will not be changed after registration.

### Missing data

Structured features are mostly observed. The average fraction of missing clinical numeric values is 9.4% (ventilation), 8.9% (RRT) and 10.3% (all-source ICU death). Very few rows have every clinical value missing (11, 11 and 270 respectively). Nonmissing fractions for key features are 97.9 to 98.9% for heart rate and MAP, 95.6 to 98.7% for creatinine and WBC, and 89.1 to 93.2% for urine output. Verbal GCS is lower (64 to 93%) because airway-coded responses are set to missing. FiO2 is available for 9.9% of the ventilation cohort and about 44% of the RRT and death cohorts. Lactate, bilirubin and pH are available less often. About 40% of MetaVision rows have an eligible note. Rows without a note are retained. They have has_note = 0 and missing semantic scores. Handling: no rows are excluded for missing predictors, so the expected sample sizes equal the cohort sizes below. The primary gradient-boosting models handle missing values natively. For logistic models, missing values are imputed with the training-fold median and the same values are applied to the held-out fold. A note-available row with an incomplete set of eight semantic scores is treated as an inference failure and stops the analysis rather than being imputed.

### Unit of analysis

The unit of analysis is the ICU stay at a 12-hour landmark. Inclusion: age 18 or older, not a neonatal ICU stay, still in the ICU at the landmark, and no disqualifying evidence of the outcome at or before the landmark. Cases have the outcome after the landmark and within the next 12 hours. Controls have no outcome and no disqualifying evidence through the horizon, and remain in the ICU through the horizon. The confirmatory analyses use MetaVision stays only. A patient may contribute more than one ICU stay; all cross-validation and bootstrap resampling are grouped by patient. Of 61,522 MIMIC-III ICU stays, 53,329 met the adult and non-neonatal criteria (38,510 patients). Expected analysis sizes: Invasive ventilation: 11,116 stays, 8,982 patients, 279 cases RRT: 19,395 stays, 15,080 patients, 314 cases ICU death, MetaVision: 19,811 stays, 15,312 patients, 214 cases ICU death, CareVue replication: 25,632 stays, 19,736 patients, 306 cases

### Statistical outliers

No observations are removed as statistical outliers. Physiologically implausible values are set to missing before modeling using fixed plausibility ranges: heart rate 0 to 300, systolic pressure 0 to 400, diastolic and mean pressure 0 to 300, respiratory rate 0 to 70, SpO2 0 to 100, temperature 10 to 50 °C, GCS components within their scale ranges, FiO2 20 to 100% after unit normalization, laboratory values above 0 and below lactate 50, creatinine 150, BUN 300, WBC 1000, hemoglobin 50, platelets 10,000, sodium 200, potassium 30, total bilirubin 150 and INR 50 (bicarbonate, chloride and glucose below 10,000), and pH between 6.0 and 8.5. These rules were fixed before any outcome modeling. In the extraction, 37,315 charted rows and 39 laboratory rows were set to missing. Expected sample sizes are unchanged.

### Sampling weights

No sampling weights exist for MIMIC-III and none are used. Results describe the defined MIMIC-III landmark cohorts and are not reweighted to any other population.

### Prior knowledge

The author (shared project history). The author has worked with MIMIC-III v1.4 and the Zigong v1.1 database first-hand in the earlier phase of this project (v1). The following results were seen before this registration and informed some design choices, as stated in the attached analysis plan. v1 matched case-control analyses (MIMIC-III): In a 6-hour vasopressor-initiation study, Open-Jev and Laya semantic scores added +0.0148 and +0.0107 AUROC over linear structured physiology. In a matched 12-hour benchmark, increments over structured physiology were +0.021 (ventilation), +0.0005 (RRT) and +0.021 (ICU death) for Open-Jev, with larger ventilation and death increments for DiffusionGemma. TF-IDF of note text generally outperformed the eight semantic scores, which added little once TF-IDF was included. A supervised text encoder reached locked-test AUROCs of 0.721, 0.808 and 0.922 for the three outcomes. v1 population landmark analysis (MIMIC-III): Structured-only AUROCs were 0.707 (ventilation), 0.948 (RRT) and 0.793 (ICU death). Open-Jev increments over structured physiology plus note context were +0.0034, -0.0008 and +0.0133, and Laya increments were -0.0047, -0.0003 and +0.0072. Adding note context raised ventilation AUROC from 0.707 to 0.860; this was later traced to source-system confounding. v1 external analyses: A frozen structured vasopressor model gave approximate AUROCs of 0.720 in eICU and 0.663 in NWICU. A DiffusionGemma semantic model trained on MIMIC-III gave AUROC 0.591 (95% CI 0.514 to 0.668) for 24-hour invasive ventilation in 85 Zigong cases and 255 controls. Integrity review: An external code review found errors that affected the v1 results. These were ventilation outcome observability tied to the CareVue/MetaVision source system, outcome-language filtering that changed note availability, exclusion of Physician and Respiratory notes because of trailing whitespace in category labels, missed error flags, inconsistent aggregation of semantic chunk scores, an eICU laboratory-window error, and an over-broad Zigong leakage filter. All v1 results are treated as exploratory provenance and are not carried forward as estimates. Descriptive, outcome-stratified information seen during the v2.1 redesign (no predictive models fitted on real outcome labels): case and control counts for each cohort; note coverage by outcome status; the share of case and control notes containing direct treatment or endpoint language (for example, 54.6% of note-available RRT case notes versus 3.6% of control notes before stripping); and feature missingness by cohort. A label-free audit showed that CareVue and MetaVision stays could be distinguished from the comparator features (source-prediction AUROC 0.98). This led to the MetaVision-only confirmatory design. An earlier structured-only v2 evaluation run timed out before producing results; its partial outputs were quarantined unread. The runtime benchmarks used synthetic data only. No v2.1 predictive performance (structured, semantic or lexical) on real outcome labels had been computed or seen at the time of registration. Knowledge from the literature: The author is aware of published work showing that nursing documentation patterns and nurse concern predict deterioration (for example, the CONCERN trial, Rossetti et al., Nature Medicine 2025) and that clinical notes can improve ICU outcome prediction in MIMIC-III.

## Analyses

### Statistical models

H1 to H3 (primary). For each outcome separately, two gradient-boosted tree classifiers are compared (scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingClassifier; learning rate 0.05, 300 iterations, at most 15 leaf nodes, minimum 50 samples per leaf, L2 regularization 1.0, no early stopping; the same settings for all outcomes, no tuning). Comparator model: 34 structured physiology features + treatment and support context (+ code status for ICU death) + documentation-behavior metadata + note context (has_note, selected-note age, collapsed category). Augmented model: the same model plus the eight stripped-note Open-Jev scores. Rows without a note keep missing semantic values, which the model handles natively. No explicit interaction terms are specified; the tree model can learn interactions. Both models are fitted by five-fold cross-fitting with patient-grouped, outcome-stratified folds (primary partition seed 20260924). Held-out predictions from all folds are pooled. The primary estimand is delta AUROC = AUROC(augmented) minus AUROC(comparator). H4. As H1 to H3, using the six state-dominant constructs instead of all eight. H5. Lexical comparison in a common L2-penalized logistic regression family, fitted on the same folds. Four models are compared: comparator; comparator + Open-Jev; comparator + TF-IDF; comparator + TF-IDF + Open-Jev. TF-IDF uses the same stripped note (unigrams and bigrams, at most 10,000 features, min_df 5, max_df 0.98, sublinear term frequency, fitted within training folds). Rows without a note receive an all-zero TF-IDF vector. Missing Open-Jev scores are set to the training-fold median; has_note remains in the comparator. H6. The primary HGB comparison is repeated with the unstripped note, Laya or DiffusionGemma scores instead of Open-Jev, the broad respiratory-support ventilation endpoint, the prespecified timing restrictions, and CareVue ICU-death stays analysed separately. H7. The primary comparison is repeated after permuting complete eight-score vectors among note-available patients within deciles of comparator out-of-fold risk (seed 20260929). No-note rows are unchanged. H8. Blinded clinicians rate the eight constructs in a random sample of frozen notes. We report inter-rater reliability, same-construct agreement between model scores and clinician ratings, agreement across different constructs, and the association between model scores and clinicians' 12-hour deterioration probabilities. H9. The frozen MIMIC-derived semantic models are applied without refitting to the rebuilt Zigong cohort: Open-Jev, Laya and TF-IDF on locally translated English text, and DiffusionGemma on native Chinese text. External AUROC is reported with patient-level bootstrap intervals.

### Effect size

No minimum effect size of interest is used as a decision threshold. For context, v1 semantic increments ranged from about -0.005 to +0.013 AUROC. The study is designed to estimate the size of the increment, not to test for a prespecified effect.

### Statistical power

Approximate 80% detectable delta AUROC (assumed paired prediction correlation 0.90, based on the frozen case counts and note coverage): full cohort 0.0233 (ventilation, 279 cases), 0.0113 (RRT, 314 cases), 0.0241 (MetaVision ICU death, 214 cases); note-available rows only 0.0334, 0.0170 and 0.0385. Ventilation and ICU death therefore have limited precision for increments in the range seen in v1. These values guide interpretation only.

### Inference criteria

Estimation-first. For each primary outcome we report delta AUROC with a two-sided 95% percentile interval from 500 patient-cluster refit-bootstrap replicates. In each replicate, patients are resampled with replacement, keep their primary fold assignment, and both models are refit in all five folds (child seeds from numpy.random.SeedSequence(20260924).spawn(500)). We also report delta AUROC from four further frozen partitions (seeds 20260925 to 20260928) and their range. No p-values are calculated and no multiplicity adjustment is applied. The three outcomes are separate co-primary estimands, and no outcome is declared positive or negative according to whether its interval excludes zero. Secondary metrics (delta AUPRC, Brier score, log loss, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, expected calibration error, decision-curve net benefit) are reported with patient-cluster bootstrap intervals and are descriptive.

### Assumption Violation/Model Non-Convergence

The HGB models use a fixed number of iterations, so convergence failure does not apply. If a note-available row lacks any of the eight semantic scores, the analysis stops, and the failure is logged and corrected before results are viewed. Implementation or data-integrity problems found after registration follow the deviation procedure in the SAP: the analysis stops, the problem and correction are logged with dates before corrected results are inspected, and both versions are reported if results were already seen.

### Reliability and Robustness Testing

Refit and split stability across five frozen patient-grouped partitions Patient-cluster refit bootstrap Alternative semantic instruments (Laya, DiffusionGemma) Unstripped-note sensitivity Broad respiratory-support ventilation endpoint Prospective-timing restrictions Within-source replication in CareVue ICU death, reported side by side with MetaVision without pooling Patient-shuffled negative control External validation in Zigong (translated and native-Chinese arms) Clinician construct validation All preprocessing (imputation, scaling, TF-IDF vocabulary) is fitted within training folds only.

### Exploratory analysis

The following are exploratory and will be reported under a separate heading: semantic increments among stays in the lower 80% of comparator-predicted risk, with alert-rate summaries (events detected, positive predictive value, number evaluated per additional event); subgroup calibration and semantic-score distributions by recorded ethnicity, insurance and language; note-category (author-type) differences; changes in semantic scores across successive pre-landmark notes; comparison of CareVue and MetaVision results as a documentation-system shift. Any other analysis not listed in this registration will also be labelled exploratory.

## Metadata

### Description

This registration records the analysis plan for the remaining confirmatory analyses of a study using MIMIC-III ICU data. We ask whether semantic information extracted from bedside clinical notes adds discrimination for three outcomes within 12 hours after a 12-hour ICU landmark: invasive ventilation, renal-replacement therapy, and ICU death. The comparison is made against a comparator that already includes structured physiology and laboratory values, treatment and respiratory-support state, and documentation-behavior metadata. The primary semantic representation is a set of eight clinician-interpretable scores produced by a locally run language-model instrument (Open-Jev) from a fixed pre-landmark note with direct treatment language removed. This is not an inception-stage preregistration. An earlier version of the analysis (v1) was completed and reviewed, and its results are disclosed in the attached plan.
