## Scientific question

The study asks whether prospectively available ICU bedside-note information, compressed into eight low-dimensional semantic scores, improves short-horizon deterioration discrimination beyond a strong structured EHR comparator.

The corrected v2.1 primary analyses use source-compatible MetaVision cohorts, a fixed 12-hour ICU landmark and 12-hour prediction horizon, patient-grouped frozen partitions, a rich structured comparator, and patient-cluster refit-bootstrap uncertainty.

## Post-registration explanatory analyses

The label-free alignment audit found that real hemodynamic and respiratory concern scores were more strongly associated with matching structured support states than between-patient shuffled scores. This supports correct gross score-to-stay alignment. It is not a substitute for human construct validation.

Semantic-only AUROC among note-available rows on the primary frozen partition was 0.603 for ventilation, 0.448 for RRT, and 0.657 for MetaVision ICU death.

The four-level comparator decomposition showed:

| Outcome | Delta at A: 34 physiology/lab/urine | Delta at B: + treatment/support | Delta at C: + documentation behavior | Delta at D: registered rich comparator |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Invasive ventilation | +0.02032 | +0.01137 | -0.00172 | -0.00005 |
| RRT | -0.00054 | -0.00070 | +0.00014 | -0.00079 |
| ICU death, MetaVision | -0.00428 | -0.00280 | -0.00394 | -0.00212 |

For ventilation, semantic-score missingness can reveal note availability before documentation/note-context variables enter the comparator, so the positive early-level increment cannot be assigned purely to semantic content.

## Manuscript framing

The narrow manuscript claim is:

> In preregistered corrected MIMIC-III analyses, eight low-dimensional semantic scores derived from prospectively available bedside notes did not consistently improve short-horizon deterioration discrimination beyond a strong structured EHR comparator.

The explanatory result is that bedside-note text can retain residual information under a high-dimensional lexical representation, while compression into eight predefined semantic dimensions loses predictive information and becomes largely redundant once treatment, documentation behavior, and note context are represented.
