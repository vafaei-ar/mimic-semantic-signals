## Central scientific contribution and editorial verdict

**Proposed question:** Do eight predefined, clinician-concern semantic scores extracted from pre-landmark ICU bedside notes improve short-horizon clinical deterioration prediction above a strong structured electronic health record (EHR) model that already includes physiology, treatment/support, documentation behavior and note context?

**Verdict:** A JAMIA submission remains a reasonable ambitious choice, but the generic finding that additional text or images may add little to strong structured predictors is **not novel**. Peng et al. published that finding for multimodal risk prediction in JAMIA in 2026. The publishable distinction must concern the *registered incremental-value design and fixed semantic representation*, not simply a negative result. We cannot promise acceptance or infer a submission probability.

The primary analysis estimates incremental AUROC from eight fixed Open-Jev semantic dimensions in three MetaVision ICU outcomes with five frozen patient-grouped partitions and patient-cluster full-refit bootstrap uncertainty. These clinically labeled dimensions were **not independently validated by clinician raters**. Their naming does not itself establish construct validity. A stronger baseline may contain overlapping prognostic information, but these estimates do not causally identify information redundancy.

## A. Registered primary H1-H3 evidence

All three cohorts use the 12-hour ICU-admission landmark and the subsequent 12-hour outcome horizon in MIMIC-III MetaVision. The comparator combines 34 physiologic/laboratory/urine features with treatment/support, documentation behavior, and note context. The semantic model adds eight Open-Jev scores. The primary estimand is the repeat-1 out-of-fold **augmented minus comparator AUROC**. Confidence intervals use **500 valid patient-cluster full-refit bootstrap replicates**. Other frozen partitions are stability checks.

This is a targeted PubMed-first novelty search, **not a systematic literature review**. Metadata below were checked in PubMed on 2026-11-11. Do not cite works as new evidence for our model unless the comparisons match in modality, note timing, outcome and estimand.

**Novelty assessment:** The relevant advance is not that structured EHR data can already be highly predictive. It is the *rigorous incremental-value evaluation of a small, predefined semantic representation after a documentation-aware baseline*, including explicit registration, source-patient grouping, multiple outcomes, robust uncertainty and negative controls. This remains a narrower contribution than a new clinically validated instrument or prospectively proven decision system.

## D. JAMIA paper architecture and risks

**Working title:** *Incremental predictive value of predefined semantic scores from ICU notes beyond documentation-aware EHR models: a preregistered evaluation*.

**Working thesis:** The eight fixed semantic scores did not demonstrate reliable additional discrimination above a rich structured and documentation-aware comparator across three registered ICU deterioration outcomes. The result motivates careful comparator design when judging compact text representations. It does **not** establish causal redundancy, statistical equivalence or that all narrative representations lack utility.

The Results should first present registered H1-H3 and their full-refit intervals, then the registered calibration/proper-scoring/sensitivity evidence. Later supervised embeddings, lexical scores, early fusion and comparator richness should be clearly labeled exploratory and mostly supplementary. The Discussion must acknowledge Peng 2026, clinician-validation failure, the incomplete independent narrative external validation, no-note structural missingness and the unusual ventilation thin-comparator percentile distribution.

**Likely desk-rejection concerns:** overlap with Peng 2026, MIMIC-III single-center data, near-null results without demonstrated clinical outcome benefit, no clinician construct validation, no successful planned external validation, and a long exploratory extension narrative. The manuscript cannot repair these scientific limits with optimistic language.

**Next figure and table planning boundary:** Main Table 1 should present cohorts and note availability. Main Table 2 should present the registered three-outcome ΔAUROC and full-refit intervals, possibly alongside secondary calibration. Figure 1 should explain the fixed landmark, eligible-note selection, semantic representation and rich structured comparator. Figure 2 should plot registered incremental discrimination and uncertainty directly from frozen aggregate results. Registered sensitivity checks and post-registration decomposition should receive clearly marked supplemental plots/tables. A separate code-backed figure inventory must determine which aggregate results can be reproduced faithfully before final rendering.
